How We Rate

How this directory works. Real Psychic Ratings brings together publicly displayed advisor information so you can compare choices. A profile rating is a platform-reported figure at the time it was collected; it is not a prediction-accuracy score or proof that we tested that advisor. Prices, availability and offers can change. Check the advisor’s current listing before paying.

What you can compare

Start with the question you want to discuss, then compare advisors in that specialty. Profile pages may show a displayed rating, review count, stated experience, languages, communication style, tools and a price per minute. Each field answers a different question. A high displayed score with very few reviews gives less context than a score supported by a larger body of feedback. A listed price is useful for a budget estimate, but the checkout screen is the source for the amount you will actually pay.

How to use a displayed rating

  1. Read the source. Open the original platform listing when a source link is available. Look for the date and number of reviews, not only the average.
  2. Check recent feedback. Comments about communication, clarity and billing are more useful than vague claims about supernatural accuracy.
  3. Compare like with like. An email response, a ten-minute chat and a long phone call provide different experiences. Format and rate matter alongside feedback.
  4. Confirm before purchase. Advisor availability, price, trial terms, deposit requirements and refund rules are set by the platform and may change without notice.

What we do not verify

We do not independently verify paranormal abilities, predictions, the identity behind every profile, or the authenticity of every third-party review. We cannot guarantee that a reading will be accurate or helpful. A rating shown here should be treated as a research signal, not an endorsement. The directory can contain missing or stale fields. If information conflicts with an advisor’s current platform page, use the platform’s current terms.

Our practical selection checklist

Write one or two open questions. Select the reading format you prefer. Compare at least three profiles on the same platform or across platforms. Calculate your maximum session cost as the displayed per-minute rate multiplied by the number of minutes you plan to use, then include any deposit or tax shown at checkout. Set a timer and stop when you reach your limit. Never share passwords, banking credentials or other sensitive information with an advisor.

For a medical, legal, financial or mental-health decision, use a qualified professional. A psychic reading is a personal or entertainment experience and should not replace professional advice.

Reading a profile field by field

A profile is a bundle of separate claims, and each one deserves its own level of trust. Some fields are easy to check, such as a listed language or whether a chat option exists. Others are self-reported, such as years of experience or a description of style. A few are generated by the platform, such as a displayed average or a count of completed sessions. Treat the first group as practical filters, the second as the advisor’s own marketing, and the third as data whose method you usually cannot see.

  • Displayed rating: a summary of feedback gathered by the platform. Ask how it is calculated and whether old reviews still count.
  • Review count: the size of the sample behind that rating. A small count can swing widely with one or two comments.
  • Stated experience: the advisor’s own description. It is rarely checked by an outside party.
  • Specialties and tools: useful for matching a topic, but they are labels the advisor chooses.
  • Per-minute rate: a starting figure that can differ from the final charge once offers, taxes and deposits apply.

Why we avoid a single league table

A numbered ranking suggests a precision that this subject cannot support. Nobody can measure the accuracy of a psychic reading in a way that lets you compare two advisors on a shared scale. What can be compared is how clearly a platform explains its terms, how easily you can control your spending, how responsive its support appears to be and how much usable detail an advisor profile offers. Those are consumer questions, and that is where our attention goes.

For the same reason, we do not turn displayed ratings into a score of our own. Combining numbers from different platforms would hide the fact that each platform collects feedback differently. Some invite a rating after every session, while others ask only occasionally. A figure that looks identical on two sites can mean quite different things.

What can go stale, and how to protect yourself

Directory data ages quickly. Advisors change their rates, go offline, join other platforms or leave. Promotions start and end without notice. If a page here shows a figure, treat it as a snapshot of what was published when it was gathered. Before you commit, open the source listing and look for four things in this order: whether the advisor is currently available, the rate shown today, the date of the most recent reviews, and the cancellation or credit terms.

Signals that deserve extra caution

  • A flawless score supported by only a handful of reviews.
  • Reviews that repeat the same phrases or praise results in the future tense, such as “she said I would”.
  • Profiles that promise guaranteed outcomes or claim an exact success rate.
  • Offers that hide the post-introduction rate or require a payment method before showing it.
  • Advisors who steer you away from the platform’s own payment and messaging tools.

None of these proves misconduct on its own. Together they are a reason to slow down, choose a different profile, or contact the platform’s support team with questions before spending anything.

How you can help keep the directory accurate

Readers often notice problems before editors do. If you see a rate that has changed, a listing that has disappeared or a description that reads as misleading, tell us through the contact page and include the profile address and a link to the current source. Corrections are checked against published provider terms, never against anonymous claims alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is a higher rating always better?

Not necessarily. A higher number with few reviews can be less informative than a slightly lower number built on many recent reviews. Topic fit, price and communication style matter as much as the score.

Do you accept payment to change a rating?

Displayed ratings come from platform data and are not edited for sponsors. Read the editorial policy for how commercial links are handled.

Can I rely on a rating if I cannot find the source?

Treat it as a weak signal. Without the original listing you cannot check the date, the count or the wording of recent feedback.
