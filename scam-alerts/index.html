Scam Alerts

A useful reading should never require fear, secrecy or an open-ended payment commitment. The checks below help you spot pressure tactics before they become expensive.

Common warning signs

  • Curse removal for an extra fee. Someone claims you or a loved one is cursed, then demands repeated payments to remove it. You cannot verify that claim, and the promised result is often impossible to measure.
  • Guaranteed relationship or financial outcomes. No advisor can guarantee that someone will return, that a job will be offered or that an investment will succeed.
  • Urgency and escalating minutes. A reader says you must continue immediately or lose your only chance. Pause, end the call and compare what was promised with the platform’s rules.
  • Off-platform money or sensitive data. Requests for direct transfers, passwords, bank details or identity documents create avoidable risk. Use only the provider’s normal payment and support channels.
  • Unsolicited private contact. Be cautious if someone contacts you unexpectedly and claims to know about a crisis or secret problem.

Protect your time and budget

Before a session, choose a maximum spend and calculate the minutes it will buy at the current rate. Keep one or two questions at hand and use a timer. Save a screenshot of the listed rate and offer terms. If the conversation becomes vague or repetitive, you can stop without paying for more time to get the same answer.

If something goes wrong

  1. Stop the session and do not send further money.
  2. Save receipts, messages, the advisor name, date and advertised terms.
  3. Contact the platform’s official support and ask for its complaint or resolution procedure.
  4. If you shared account credentials, change them and secure the affected account. For a disputed card charge, contact your payment provider.

How these schemes usually unfold

Consumer-protection bodies and security writers describe a fairly consistent pattern. It starts with a small, inexpensive or free contact that builds trust. Then comes a revelation: you are cursed, a loved one is in danger, or a hidden obstacle is blocking your luck. The solution is always a paid ritual or a premium session, and when you pay, a new problem appears. The amounts can rise sharply over weeks or months. Knowing the sequence helps because the first step looks harmless.

Additional red flags to watch for

  • Fear as a sales tool: predictions of illness, accidents or death unless you buy something.
  • Fostering dependence: insistence that you cannot make decisions without checking with the reader first.
  • Requests for secrecy: being told not to discuss the matter with relatives or friends.
  • Unusual payment methods: gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers or peer-to-peer apps with instructions to label payments as gifts.
  • Pressure to move off the platform: a switch to a private messaging app or an outside payment link.
  • Flattery paired with vague details: remarks that could fit anyone, followed by a request for money to unlock more.
  • Demands to “cleanse” money or belongings: particularly handing over cash or jewelry for safekeeping.
  • Fake urgency: an offer that will vanish in minutes or a warning that the window to act is closing.

Who is most at risk

Anyone can be misled, but bad actors often look for people in a vulnerable moment: grief, loneliness, a breakup, a health scare or financial stress. If you are in such a period, set rules for yourself in advance, such as a monthly spending limit and a rule that you will never pay in the heat of a session.

Unsolicited approaches

Messages that arrive uninvited deserve special suspicion. Examples include an email or social media message from a stranger claiming to have a vision about you, a letter promising lottery numbers, or a pop-up saying a reading has been “reserved” for you. Real services have no reason to know your private worries before you contact them. Do not click links or reply, and report the message to the platform or your national consumer agency.

Checking a provider before you pay

  1. Search the business name together with words such as “complaints” or “refund”.
  2. Look for a physical or registered business address and a working support route.
  3. Read the terms for cancellation, refunds and recurring charges.
  4. Confirm that the checkout page is secure and that the price matches the listing.
  5. Be careful with subscriptions that renew automatically after an introductory period.

Reporting and recovery

Report the incident to the platform, then to your card issuer if the charge is disputable. Many countries have a national consumer protection agency or a fraud reporting center that takes complaints about deceptive sales practices. Keep records, avoid paying anyone who offers to recover your money for a fee, and be aware that scammers sometimes return to earlier victims with a “recovery” offer. If you feel shame, remember that these schemes are designed by people who study human psychology, and being targeted says nothing about your intelligence.

Frequently asked questions

Are all online psychics scams?

No. Many people use legitimate platforms with clear pricing and complaint processes. The warning signs above describe conduct, not a whole industry.

What if I already paid for a curse removal?

Stop further payments, save the evidence and contact your card issuer and the platform. Do not pay again to undo the earlier payment.

Can I trust a reader who refuses to take payment?

Be careful. An offer of a free reading can be the first step of the sequence described above, so look at what follows.

Keep the right support close

For a medical, legal, financial or mental-health issue, seek a qualified professional. If you are grieving, a counselor or trusted support network can offer care that a reading may not provide. No advisor should pressure you to stop treatment or professional help.
